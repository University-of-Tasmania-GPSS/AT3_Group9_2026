## Why Map Crime?

Crime does not occur uniformly across cities. Understanding where offences are concentrated, and how these patterns vary between communities, can help reveal important spatial differences in public safety. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide tools to map these patterns, identify crime hotspots and investigate how crime rates vary across different areas.

## Project Overview

This project uses GIS to investigate the spatial distribution of recorded crime across selected Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Greater Sydney between 2015 and 2025. It identifies areas with higher concentrations of crime and explores whether crime rates are associated with socioeconomic characteristics, including unemployment, household income and high-school completion.

By combining crime data with socioeconomic information, the project aims to provide a clearer picture of how crime patterns vary geographically. These relationships are explored as spatial associations rather than evidence that socioeconomic conditions directly cause crime.

## Aims and Objectives

The project has four main objectives:

1. Map the spatial distribution of recorded crime across selected Greater Sydney LGAs.
2. Identify and visualise crime hotspots and areas with comparatively lower crime rates.
3. Explore spatial associations between crime rates and selected socioeconomic indicators.
4. Communicate the findings through GIS maps, visualisations and spatial analysis.

## Why Does This Matter?

Understanding where crime is concentrated can help inform discussions about community safety, crime prevention and the allocation of public resources. Investigating socioeconomic patterns alongside crime data may also help identify areas that warrant further research and community support. The findings will provide a spatial overview of crime across Greater Sydney while recognising that recorded crime data have limitations and that spatial relationships do not necessarily establish causation.
